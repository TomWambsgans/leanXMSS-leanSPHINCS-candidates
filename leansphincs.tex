\section{The scheme}

Same shape as SLH-DSA-128-24 from NIST's recent SP~800-230~\cite{SP800230} (single XMSS layer), with the differences:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $h = 26$, instead of $22$ (tree height, i.e. height of the only XMSS layer)
    \item $k = 24$, instead of $6$ (number of trees per FORS)
    \item $a = 10$, instead of $24$ (height of each FORS tree)
    \item WOTS$^+$C~\cite{HK22C}, instead of WOTS. 64 chains of length $w=4$, with a target sum of 120
  \end{itemize}

Properties:

\begin{itemize}
  \item public key: 32 bytes
  \item secret key: 32 bytes
  \item signature size: 5\,684 bytes
  \item verification: 391 compressions (with BLAKE2s)
  \item security: NIST level 1 \cite{FIPS205} (applies to all the usages below) (127 bits SUF in the ROM, and $\approx 64$ bits in the QROM)
  \item lifetime: up to $2^{30}$ signatures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Usage}

\subsection{Full \texorpdfstring{$2^{30}$}{2\textasciicircum 30} lifetime}

For powerful devices.

\begin{itemize}
  \item lifetime: $2^{30}$
  \item keygen: $19 \cdot 10^9$ compressions
  \item signing 
    \begin{itemize}
    \item with a 1~MiB cache: $421\cdot10^3$ compressions
    \item with a 1~GiB cache: $75\cdot10^3$ compressions
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\emph{Modern laptop CPUs can compute on the order of $10^9$ SHA2 or BLAKE2s compressions per second.}


\subsection{Pruned: \texorpdfstring{$2^{17}$}{2\textasciicircum 17} lifetime}\label{sec:pruned}

For weaker devices. The trick is to trade smaller lifetime for faster keygen, using hypertree pruning~\cite{conduition}: only build one subtree of height 13, and replace the 13 siblings above it by pseudorandom surrogate nodes.

\begin{itemize}
  \item lifetime: $2^{17}$
  \item keygen: $2.2\cdot10^6$ compressions
  \item signing: $111\cdot10^3$ compressions, with a 16~KiB cache (including $33\cdot10^3$ compressions of grinding)
\end{itemize}

\emph{A Ledger Nano S+ can compute $33$ (resp $25$) thousand BLAKE2s (resp SHA2) per second.}

\subsection{Pruned: \texorpdfstring{$2^{24}$}{2\textasciicircum 24} lifetime}

For laptops, phones and powerful hardware wallets. The same pruning, with a subtree of height 20 (and 6
surrogate siblings).

\begin{itemize}
  \item lifetime: $2^{24}$
  \item keygen: $287\cdot10^6$ compressions
  \item signing: $84\cdot10^3$ compressions, with a 1~MiB cache (including 256 compressions of grinding)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Threshold signing}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $t$-of-$n$
  \item signing: malicious security against $t-1$ operators (with abort); 400 bytes per signer, 2 rounds
  \item keygen: no trusted dealer, a one-time MPC among the $n$ operators, fewer than $n/2$ corrupt
  \item lifetime: $2^L$, e.g.\ $L=12$, 14 or 16 (pruned, subtree of height $L-4$)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Idea (adapted from PRAWNS~\cite{PRAWNS}).} Each FORS secret is $F(\mathit{addr})$, for a
threshold PRF $F$: $t$ operators evaluate it together, $t-1$ learn nothing (context-aware, see below).
\begin{itemize}
  \item keygen (MPC): computes and publishes all FORS leaves $\hash(F(\mathit{addr}))$, and the signature of
        each FORS root by its WOTS key
  \item signing: the $t$ signers evaluate $F$ at the 24 FORS addresses selected by the message; everything
        else (FORS paths, WOTS signature, authentication path) is read from the public data
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Choice of $F$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item hash-based, for small $\binom{n}{t-1}$: replicated seeds~\cite{CDI05}, one seed $k_T$ per set $T$ of $t-1$
        operators, held by the others; $F(x)=\bigoplus_T\hash(k_T,x)$. Hash only
  \item lattice-based, for large $\binom{n}{t-1}$: threshold PRF~\cite{PRAWNS},
        $F(x)=\lfloor\langle\hash(x),\mathit{msk}\rangle\rceil_p$, $\mathit{msk}$ Shamir-shared. LWE
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Context-awareness~\cite{PRAWNS}.} To reveal $F(x)$, each of the $t$ signers sends a part $p_i$,
and the parts combine into $F(x)$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item hash-based: within the quorum, each term $\hash(k_T,x)$ is assigned to one signer holding $k_T$; $p_i$ is
        the XOR of its terms, and $F(x)=\bigoplus_i p_i$
  \item lattice-based: $p_i=\lambda_i\langle\hash(x),\mathit{sk}_i\rangle$ (Lagrange coefficient $\lambda_i$), and
        $F(x)$ is $\sum_i p_i \bmod q$, rounded
\end{itemize}
A signing can abort. Sent as is, parts of $F(x)$ gathered from aborted signings of different messages
could be combined into $F(x)$, revealing a FORS secret no signature revealed. So each part is bound to its
message $m$: signer $i$ adds a mask $\hash(k_{ij},x,m)$ for each co-signer $j$, with $k_{ij}$ a key shared by
$i$ and $j$. Within one signing, each mask is added twice (by $i$ and by $j$) and cancels; parts from
signings of different messages carry different masks and don't combine.

\paragraph{Keygen.} Honest-majority MPC, malicious with abort:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item coin tossing: $P$, the kept subtree, its $30-L$ surrogates
  \item the key of $F$: hash-based, each seed by commit-reveal among its holders; lattice-based, a
        verifiable Shamir sharing of $\mathit{msk}$
  \item MPC: $F$ at the $2^{L-4}\cdot24\cdot2^{10}$ FORS addresses, hashed into leaves; $F$ at the
        $2^{L-4}\cdot64$ WOTS chain starts, hashed 3 times. Open the leaves and chain ends
  \item in the clear: FORS roots, WOTS$^+$C counters; open the WOTS signatures; build the tree
  \item the operators check they all got the same public data (public key, FORS leaves, WOTS signatures;
        1.6~GB for $L=16$) by comparing its hash. Each keeps the public data and its key share of $F$, and
        deletes everything else
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Signing (any $t$).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item agree on $m$
  \item $R_0=F(\mathsf{msg},m)$; grind $R=\hash(R_0,\mathit{ctr})$ into the kept subtree. Deterministic: all
        quorums give the same signature
  \item open $F$ at the 24 FORS addresses; check each against its leaf
  \item output $R$, the 24 secrets and paths, the WOTS signature, the authentication path
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Keygen cost (measured).} $\approx1.5\cdot10^3\cdot2^L$ MPC hashes (mostly FORS leaves) of
$\approx13.5\cdot10^3$ AND gates. The MPC uses replicated sharing over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ as
in~\cite{BGIN19}, generalized to $n=2f+1$: each AND gate is computed locally and reshared through one
collector, $(n-1)+f$ bits in total, i.e.\ 1, 1.2 and 1.29 bits per operator for $n=3$, 5, 7, plus a
check~\cite{GS20} of negligible cost. Traffic per operator:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
  \toprule
  & $2^{12}$ & $2^{14}$ & $2^{16}$ \\
  \midrule
  2-of-3 & 11~GB & 43~GB & 172~GB \\
  3-of-5 & 13~GB & 52~GB & 207~GB \\
  4-of-7 & 14~GB & 55~GB & 222~GB \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Remarks.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item With a trusted dealer instead, keygen needs no MPC: the dealer computes everything in the clear
        ($3\cdot10^8$ compressions for $L=16$) and gives each operator its key share of $F$.
  \item The key shares can be refreshed, and the committee changed (including $n$ and $t$), with the same
        public key:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item hash-based:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item the new committee generates fresh seeds for its own $n'$, $t'$ (as at keygen): they define a
            new PRF $F'$
      \item for each FORS address $x$, $t$ old operators send their parts of $F(x)$ and the new operators
            their parts of $F'(x)$, masked so that only $c(x)=F(x)\oplus F'(x)$ is revealed; $c(x)$ is
            published (it reveals nothing: $F'(x)$ masks $F(x)$)
      \item from then on, the secret at $x$ is $F'(x)\oplus c(x)$, which equals $F(x)$: signing uses $F'$
            and the public $c$
      \item the old seeds are deleted
    \end{enumerate}
    \item lattice-based: $t$ old operators reshare $\mathit{msk}$ to the new committee, with threshold $t'$
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
