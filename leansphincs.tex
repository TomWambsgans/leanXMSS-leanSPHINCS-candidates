\section{The scheme}

Same shape as SLH-DSA-128-24 from NIST's recent SP~800-230~\cite{SP800230} (single XMSS layer), with the differences:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $h = 26$, instead of $22$ (tree height, i.e. height of the only XMSS layer)
    \item $k = 24$, instead of $6$ (number of FORS trees)
    \item $a = 10$, instead of $24$ (height of each FORS tree)
    \item WOTS$^+$C~\cite{HK22C}, instead of WOTS, with a target sum of 120
  \end{itemize}

Properties:

\begin{itemize}
  \item public key: 32 bytes
  \item secret key: 32 bytes
  \item signature size: 5\,684 bytes
  \item verification: 391 compressions (with BLAKE2s)
  \item security: NIST level 1 \cite{FIPS205} (applies to both usage cited bellow) (127 bits SUF in the ROM, and $\approx 64$ bits in the QROM)
  \item lifetime: up to $2^{30}$ signatures.
\end{itemize}

\section{Usage}

\subsection{Full \texorpdfstring{$2^{30}$}{2\textasciicircum 30} lifetime}

\begin{itemize}
  \item keygen: $19 \cdot 10^9$ compressions
  \item signing 
    \begin{itemize}
    \item with a 1~MiB cache: $421\cdot10^3$ compressions
    \item with a 1~GiB cache: $75\cdot10^3$ compressions
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pruned}

For weak signers, using hypertree pruning~\cite{conduition}: only build one subtree of height $b$, chosen
pseudorandomly from the secret, and replace the $26-b$ siblings above it by pseudorandom surrogate nodes.
To sign, grind the randomizer until the leaf index falls in the subtree: $2^{26-b}$ tries of 4
compressions each. The verifier doesn't see any difference. Only $2^b$ FORS instances are used, so the
lifetime becomes $2^{30.1-(26-b)}$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccccc@{}}
\toprule
$b$ & lifetime & keygen & signing & of which grinding\\
\midrule
12 & $2^{16.1}$ & 1.12M & 140K & 66K\\
14 & $2^{18.1}$ & 4.49M & 91K & 16K\\
16 & $2^{20.1}$ & 18.0M & 79K & 4K\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Threshold (MPC).} Pruned keys also make threshold signing practical, with an unchanged verifier.
\begin{itemize}
  \item setting: $n$ operators, at most $f<n/2$ malicious (e.g.\ 3-of-4, $f=1$), honest-majority MPC with
        malicious security. Secrecy holds against $f$ colluders (not $2f$)
  \item shares: each secret derivation $\Th(P,\tw,S)$ becomes $\bigoplus_{\mathcal T}\Th(P,\tw,k_{\mathcal T})$,
        with one seed per set $\mathcal T$ of $f$ operators, known to everyone outside
        $\mathcal T$~\cite{CDI05}. Shares are computed locally
  \item keygen: MPC only on the WOTS chains of the $2^b$ kept leaves; the rest of the tree in the clear
  \item signing: grind in the clear. On the first use of a FORS instance, MPC hashes its 24\,576 secrets
        into leaves, which are then public and cached. Then open the WOTS chain values and the 24 FORS
        secrets, each checked against public data
  \item cost ($b=12$): a BLAKE2s compression is about 14.7K AND gates, and malicious 3- or 4-party
        protocols cost about 1--2 bits per AND gate per operator~\cite{BGIN19,FF21}. That gives about
        1.5--2.2 GB per operator for keygen (786K compressions) and 46--69 MB per FORS instance on first
        use. Caveat: batched consistency checks were recently shown insecure in some of these
        protocols~\cite{BS26}, and the fixed versions may cost more
  \item bigger clusters (e.g.\ 10-of-15) need Shamir sharing: roughly 5--20$\times$ more per operator
\end{itemize}
