\begin{itemize}
  \item Same shape as SLH-DSA-128-24 from NIST's recent SP~800-230~\cite{SP800230}: a single XMSS tree,
        where each WOTS key signs a FORS instance. Height $h=26$ (instead of 22), and FORS has 24 trees
        (instead of 6), each of height 10 (instead of 24).
  \item WOTS is replaced by WOTS$^+$C~\cite{HK22C}: $w=4$, 64 chains, no checksum, digits summing to
        $T=120$.
  \item 127 bits of classical security (ROM), up to $2^{30}$ signatures.
  \item Public key 32 B, signature 5\,684 B.
  \item Verification: 391 BLAKE2s compressions, the same for every signature.
  \item Full key: 18.4G compressions to generate, about 421K to sign with a 1~MiB cache.
  \item Pruned key ($2^{16}$ signatures): 1.12M to generate, about 140K to sign.
  \item Threshold signing (e.g.\ 3-of-4) works with a small MPC key generation, and the verifier is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\section{The scheme}

Everything is hashed with BLAKE2s, truncated to 128 bits, with a 16-byte tweak and a 16-byte public
parameter $P$ in front: $\Th(P,\tw,M)=\hash(\tw\concat P\concat M)_{[0,128)}$. Costs count BLAKE2s
compressions (64-byte blocks), so a chain step, a Merkle node or a secret derivation costs~1.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Merkle tree & $h=26$, $d=1$ & $2^{26}$ WOTS$^+$C leaves\\
WOTS$^+$C & $w=4$, 64 chains, $T=120$ & digits must sum to $T$, found with a 32-bit counter\\
FORS & $a=10$, $k=24$ & plain FORS, 24 trees of 1024 leaves\\
Digest & 266 bits & 26-bit leaf index, then $24\times10$-bit FORS indices\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{WOTS$^+$C.} No checksum chains: the signer grinds a counter $c$ until the 64 base-4 digits of
$\Th(P,\tw_{\mathrm{enc}},M\concat c)$ sum to $T=120$. That takes 829 tries on average (9.7 bits). The signer
walks 120 chain steps, the verifier 72, always. A larger $T$ saves verifier steps but costs grinding
quickly (14.7K tries for $T=128$). Each WOTS key only ever signs its own FORS public key, a fixed value, so
reusing a leaf never signs two different messages.

\paragraph{Digest.} $D_b=\hash(\tw_{\mathrm{msg}}(b)\concat P\concat\rho\concat\mathit{root}\concat m)$
for $b=0,1$ gives 512 bits, of which we use 266. The leaf index $\idx$ is the low 26 bits and comes from
$D_0$ alone. Then come the 24 FORS indices.

\paragraph{Signature and costs.}
$\rho\,(16)\concat\text{FORS}\,(24\cdot11\cdot16)\concat c\,(4)\concat\text{WOTS}\,(64\cdot16)\concat
\text{path}\,(26\cdot16)=5\,684$ bytes. The public key is $(\mathit{root},P)$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Verify:} $4+264+7+1+72+17+26=391$ compressions: digest, FORS leaves and paths, FORS roots,
        WOTS encoding, chains, WOTS leaf, Merkle path.
  \item \textbf{Keygen:} $2^{26}\times274$ = 18.4G. A WOTS leaf costs 273 and its Merkle node 1.
  \item \textbf{Sign:} cache tree level 10 ($2^{16}$ nodes, 1~MiB). A signature then rebuilds the $2^{10}$
        leaves under its node (281K), hashes the cache up for the upper path (66K), and builds the FORS
        instance (74K). Total about 421K.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why these parameters.} Pruned signers (below) pay for one FORS instance per signature. The
target of about $2^{17}$ for that forces small trees, $a=10$, and then $k=24$ is the smallest $k$ reaching
$2^{30}$ at $h=26$. $h=26$ balances a pruned key's keygen against its grinding. $d=1$ keeps verification
cheap and makes pruning and threshold signing simple.

\section{Security}

We claim strong unforgeability with 127 bits of classical security in the ROM: any adversary making $q$
hash calls, with at most $2^{30}$ signing queries, wins with probability at most $q/2^{127}$. That is the
statement proved in Lean for the leanVM variant~\cite{LEANVM}; the proof for this scheme is still to do.

The dominant term is FORS reuse. With $\lambda=q_s/2^{26}$ signatures per instance on average, a hash query
forges with probability $\sum_r \mathrm{Pois}(\lambda,r)(1-(1-2^{-10})^r)^{24}$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}
\toprule
$q_s$ & $2^{28}$ & $2^{29}$ & $2^{30}$ & $2^{31}$ & $2^{32}$\\
$-\log_2$ forgery per query & 159.5 & 145.7 & 129.8 & 111.9 & 92.3\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At $2^{30}$ this term, plus $2^{-128}$ for inverting $\Th$, stays below $2^{-127}$. Past $2^{30}$ it falls
by about 18 bits per doubling, so don't overuse a key.

\section{Pruned keys}

A weak signer builds only one subtree of height $b$, chosen pseudorandomly from its secret. It replaces
the $26-b$ siblings above it with pseudorandom surrogate nodes. Following conduition's hypertree
pruning~\cite{conduition}, it signs by grinding $\rho$ until $\idx$ lands in its subtree: $2^{26-b}$ tries
of 4 compressions. Only $2^b$ FORS instances are ever used, so the lifetime is $2^{30.11-(26-b)}$. Nothing
changes for the verifier.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccccc@{}}
\toprule
$b$ & lifetime & keygen & signing & of which grinding\\
\midrule
12 & $2^{16.1}$ & 1.12M & 140K & 66K\\
14 & $2^{18.1}$ & 4.49M & 91K & 16K\\
16 & $2^{20.1}$ & 18.0M & 79K & 4K\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With $d=1$, a WOTS key still only signs its own FORS key, so pruning can't make it sign two values.

\section{Threshold signing}

Setting: $n$ operators, at most $f<n/2$ malicious, malicious-secure honest-majority MPC, e.g.\ 3-of-4 with
$f=1$. Secrecy holds against $f$ colluders (not $2f$). Use a pruned key, here $b=12$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Shares.} Every secret derivation $\Th(P,\tw,S)$ becomes $\bigoplus_{\mathcal T}\Th(P,\tw,k_{\mathcal T})$
        over replicated seeds~\cite{CDI05}: one seed per set $\mathcal T$ of $f$ operators, known to everyone
        outside $\mathcal T$. Shares are computed locally, without MPC.
  \item \textbf{DKG.} An MPC runs only the WOTS chains of the $2^b$ kept leaves and opens the endpoints.
        The rest of the tree is built in the clear.
  \item \textbf{Signing.} Operators open a shared randomizer for $m$ and grind in the clear. On the first
        use of a FORS instance, an MPC hashes its 24\,576 secrets into leaves, which are opened and cached;
        later uses are free. Then they open the WOTS chain values and the 24 FORS secrets. Each opened
        value is checked against public data (endpoint or FORS leaf), so bad shares are caught. Retries
        after an abort reuse the same $\rho$, and a signing attempt counts against the lifetime even if it
        aborts.
\end{itemize}
Cost: a BLAKE2s compression is about 14.7K AND gates. Malicious 3- or 4-party protocols cost about 1--2
bits per AND gate per operator~\cite{BGIN19,FF21}. Per operator:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & MPC compressions & traffic per operator\\
\midrule
DKG ($2^{16}$ key) & 786K & 1.5--2.2 GB\\
first use of a FORS instance & 24.6K & 46--69 MB\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Larger clusters need Shamir sharing over a small field: roughly 5--20$\times$ more per operator for, say,
10-of-15, with packing (many BLAKE2s calls per share) at the low end. To go faster without touching the
verifier, prepare FORS instances in the background, move all FORS work into the DKG, or use a trusted
dealer.

\section{Open points}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Lean proof of the 127-bit claim.
  \item An MPC implementation, to replace the per-gate estimates above with measurements.
  \item A 16-bit WOTS counter would suffice and save 2 bytes.
\end{itemize}
